For verifiers

A verifier is anyone deciding whether to trust a record: a lender, a supplier, another trader. Someone shows you a bundle of records; you check it. There is nothing to install and no account to make. The check runs in your browser and talks directly to the public record; no data goes to us, because there is no us in the middle.

Check a record now

Open the checker. It runs entirely in your browser. You can also download it as a single file to keep and open offline (it still needs an internet connection at the moment you check, to reach the public lists).

Steps

  1. Ask the holder for their bundle file (a small .json file their wallet produced).
  2. Open the checker and choose that file.
  3. Read the result. For each record you see one of: genuine and valid; cancelled by the issuer (with the reason, and the holder's dispute if they filed one); withdrawn by the holder; unverifiable (the issuer's list could not be reached — neither accept nor reject, weigh it as unconfirmed); or not genuine (the seal does not match — the record was altered).

What the check does and does not do

It confirms two things: that each record is genuine (the seal matches and nothing was altered), and whether it is still valid (checked against the issuer's own public cancellation list, the holder's dispute address, and the holder's withdrawal address).

It does not judge, score, or decide for you. It shows you the facts — who vouched, what they staked, how old the record is, whether it was disputed — and leaves the decision to you. It also does not verify the issuer's declared registration number against a government registry; the record shows you that number, and checking it against your country's own public register, if the decision warrants it, is your own step. Common Credo does not verify identity.
